# Provenance of the matrix

Where the cells in `01.md` to `39.md` come from. A lookup does not need this
file. [`../sources.md`](../sources.md) gives the link, kind and date of each
source.

## How the matrix was built

This is Altshuller's classic 39-by-39 matrix, not Matrix 2003 or Matrix
2010. No single file was copied. Two transcriptions were compared cell by
cell: L1, an English spreadsheet that several public repositories carry
unchanged, and L2, the table on the Altshuller Foundation's site as two
public repositories carry it. They agree on 1,208 cells. The 40 cells where
they differ, the disputed cells, were settled by majority against L3 and,
for three cells, L4, both of unknown printed origin. A reading that repeats a
principle number, which the matrix never does, counts as a transcription
error and got no vote. In 18 further cells L3 alone differs from L1 and L2,
which agree, and the agreed reading is kept. The result has 1,248 non-empty
and 234 empty cells. The figure of 1,263 often quoted for the classic matrix
was not confirmed: no transcription reached has more than 1,248.

The Foundation's page was read directly on 2026-11-11. It presents itself as
Appendix 1 of Altshuller, *The Algorithm of Invention* (Moscow Worker, 1973).
The 1973 print was not read, so a claim about the print rests on the page.
The page holds 1,247 non-empty and 235 empty off-diagonal cells, two of the
non-empty ones corrupt (28 31 and 36 39). It equals L2 in all 40 disputed
cells. Against the matrix here 29 cells differ, all disputed: 9 in order
only, 17 in content, 2 corrupt on the page, where the matrix keeps its
reading, and 35 37, empty on the page and `1` here. In the other 11 the page
and the matrix agree. The page carries no annotation, so it does not say
whether the 17 content differences are typing errors on the page or
differences in the print. The majority rule stays. A reader who prefers the
Foundation's table takes the L2 column below.
